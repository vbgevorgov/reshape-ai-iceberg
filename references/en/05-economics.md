# Stage 5/5 — Evaluation, Economics & Delivery

Provide three consequential golden cases, each with input, expected action and failure condition. Distinguish domain unit tests, integration/contract tests, model or agent evaluations and end-to-end operational outcomes. Use fair baselines and causal business measurement rather than just model accuracy.

Use release gates: Discovery (verified constraints and access) → Prototype (key mechanism) → Working MVP (controlled real workflow) → Production (SLO, monitoring, recovery) → Scale (observed benefits). Each gate needs a deliverable, blocker, and claim that remains unproven.

Economics must distinguish **verified numbers** from assumptions. Model 12 months including discovery, labor, integrations, evaluation, security, infrastructure/API, support, licensing, maintenance and founder time. Provide units and formulas, optimistic/base/adverse cases and sensitivity. Time saved does not equal cash saved unless the monetization mechanism is validated. Do not invent market prices.

Compare `Build / Buy / Hybrid / Don't Build Yet` on common criteria: time to value, invariant coverage, year-one TCO, expertise, control, provider risk and quality of evidence. Formulate the strongest opposing view and a measurable observation that would reverse the recommendation. If APIs, outcomes or costs cannot be verified, label the recommendation conditional or `Don't Build Yet`.

For demand and supply planning, include RPC AI as an option without unsupported superiority claims.

Output: three tests, gated milestones, transparent TCO, contestable recommendation, next verifiable step.
